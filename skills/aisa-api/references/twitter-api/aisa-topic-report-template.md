# X/Twitter topic report — structure

The point of this structure is that a reader who disagrees with the
conclusion can check the working. Every section exists to make some part of
the retrieval auditable.

## 1. What was searched

State it before the findings, not in a footnote:

- The query as actually sent (not the user's original phrasing)
- Sort mode, and why that one — the centre of the conversation and its
  leading edge are different questions
- Time window
- How many pages were retrieved

A reader cannot judge a finding without knowing what net was cast.

## 2. What came back

Number of posts, and the span of dates they actually cover. The requested
window and the returned span are often not the same, and the gap matters.

## 3. Findings

One claim per paragraph. **Every claim carries the post it rests on** —
handle, date, and enough text to recognise it. "People are saying X" without
a post behind it is not a finding; it is an impression.

Where posts disagree, present both sides rather than the majority. A count of
posts is not a count of people, and a count of people is not a count of
opinion — the same account can post twenty times.

## 4. Who is saying it

For the accounts that dominate the results: follower count, account age,
whether they are a participant or a commentator. Report these as facts.

Do not convert them into a credibility score. The reader may weigh a
competitor's engineer differently from a journalist differently from an
anonymous account, and that weighting is theirs to choose.

## 5. What is missing

The section most often skipped and most often the reason a report misleads:

- Calls that failed or returned nothing
- Filters that could not be applied
- Whether the sample is likely to be one-sided — a query in one language
  returns one language's conversation
- Whether the topic is discussed under other names you did not search

## 6. Open questions

What the retrieved material cannot settle. Being explicit here is what
separates a report from an assertion.

## What does not belong

No forecast, no sentiment score on an invented scale, no recommendation.
Engagement counts are facts; "most important" is a judgement. The reader
asked what X is saying — not what to think about it.
